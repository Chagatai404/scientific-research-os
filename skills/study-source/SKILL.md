# Study Source

Read `references/SOURCE_POLICY.md`.

1. Record bibliographic/source metadata.
2. Record how the source entered the workflow:
   - independent discovery,
   - inherited project citation,
   - user-provided source,
   - citation chain,
   - targeted reconciliation search.
3. Identify why the source is credible and what role it plays:
   - evidence,
   - reference,
   - review,
   - teaching aid.
4. Separate:
   - source claim,
   - evidence/method,
   - source interpretation,
   - your interpretation.
5. Capture exact page/section/equation/figure/timestamp where possible.
6. List assumptions, system/population, regime, and validity range.
7. Identify what the source does NOT establish.
8. Mark equations that require separate derivation.
9. Link claims to active research questions/hypotheses only after extracting the source on its own terms.
10. For papers, look for available code/data and a minimal reproduction path.
11. If the source is being used to choose or justify a scientific model, check whether an independent neutral literature-discovery pass occurred first. If not, request one before treating the source set as representative.
12. If a key claim matters to research, ask the source-verifier to independently verify it. Provide the verifier the claim and source, but not your conclusion when practical.
