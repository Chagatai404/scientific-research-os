# v0.9.6 plan: a live voice tutor at a blackboard

Status: **plan only, nothing built.** Written 2026-10-17 from the researcher's brief and a read of the current voice, HTML-session and animation code. Items marked *assumed* were not decided by the researcher and can be changed.

## The brief

- A live tutor in the session, active in the **teaching blocks that follow the quizzes and probing**.
- It teaches on a **blackboard**: dark board, chalk strokes, a natural but clearly readable handwriting font, with **illustrations, formulas and animations** written and drawn as it speaks.
- It is **not a prerecorded voice**. It lectures, and accepts **live open questions**. Questions are recorded in the session as now, but are answered with **voice and board**.
- External links stay, as **supplementary material**. The tutor must explain everything on its own.
- **English first.** Turkish and other languages are secondary.
- Also open from the last release: voice quality, Chatterbox on a GPU, FSRS parameters fitted to the researcher, stale notebooks 02/03/05 in `ams-ecal-qml`, the mutation exercise on real code.

## Assumed decisions (change any of them)

1. **The lecturer is any agent (decided by the researcher, 2026-10-17).** The contract is the saved session plus the `html_sessions.py` commands (`context`, `wait`, `board-append`, `answer`, `review`), which Claude Code, Codex or any other agent that can run a command already uses; the installer already targets both. Nothing in the page, the board script or the voice layer may name a model or vendor, and the board script is plain JSON validated by the repository, so a weaker or different model gets the same errors as a strong one. An optional stdio MCP wrapper over the same commands can follow for agents that prefer tools. Cost: a spoken answer arrives after one agent turn (seconds to tens of seconds), not instantly. A local bridge that streams answers from a model API is a later option; it needs a key and a rule change.
2. **The voice provider is the decision that matters, and it is not made yet.** Voice is a swappable provider that speaks one sentence at a time; English first. The choice is made by the researcher's ear from a listening test (see "Choosing the voice" below), not from rankings.
3. **The board is always dark**, whatever the page theme.
4. **Typed questions first.** Spoken questions through local Whisper come after the loop works; microphone access inside the sandboxed iframe is unverified.

## Choosing the voice

**Why a test, not a ranking.** Public rankings found on 2026-10-17 are blog posts that disagree with each other (the same provider appears at different ratings; no single source was checked against the arena itself), and none measures this use: a long, calm lecture with formulas, in English. The live leaderboards (Artificial Analysis Speech Arena, TTS Arena) are worth opening for a first shortlist; nothing below is a verified rank.

**Criteria, in order.** (1) Sounds natural and pleasant over twenty minutes of English lecture, to the researcher. (2) Says technical words and read-out formulas correctly. (3) Starts speaking soon after a sentence is requested, because the lecture is generated and spoken sentence by sentence. (4) Licence and privacy: where the text goes, what it costs, whether it may ship in a product. (5) Works offline or degrades to the device voice. (6) Turkish and other languages, as a secondary criterion. (7) Gives timing information (sentence duration, ideally word times) so the board can follow.

| Candidate | Where it runs | Known | Not known |
|---|---|---|---|
| Browser/OS voice | device | baseline, free, offline | in-app browser here offers one old Turkish voice and no English voice |
| Edge online natural voices | Microsoft service via Edge | no key, text leaves the machine, Edge only | whether usable from a standalone page or Obsidian; terms for this use |
| Kokoro-82M | local CPU or GPU | Apache-2.0 weights per its model listings; small | speed and install on this Windows machine; espeak-ng phonemizer licence; English quality to the researcher's ear; Turkish unsupported in the base model |
| Chatterbox (GPU) | local, 6 GB card with about 1.8 GB free | MIT; sounded unnatural on CPU | GPU quality and speed; whether it fits alongside the other 4 GB in use |
| Cloud streaming voices (for example ElevenLabs, Cartesia, OpenAI, Inworld, Fish Audio) | vendor | all reported as strong and low-latency in vendor-adjacent sources | quality here, real prices (sources conflict, roughly $15 to $100 per million characters), timing output, terms; needs the researcher's own key and sends the lecture text out |

**Listening test (part of L2).** One English lecture paragraph with a formula and technical terms, rendered by every reachable candidate with the same text, shown on a page as unlabelled samples A, B, C… in random order, with a 1 to 5 rating and a note per sample, plus time-to-first-audio for each. The researcher rates blind; the labels are revealed afterwards and the result is written to `docs/plans/` like the earlier bake-off. Cloud samples are generated only with the researcher's key, on the paragraph only, and only after they say so. Local candidates are installed in their own environments, never globally, only after they approve the install.

**Status of the test (2026-10-17).** Built as `scripts/voice_blind_test.py` (7 unit tests pass). Kokoro-82M is installed in its own environment `C:\Users\ASUS\voice-envs\kokoro` with its model files in `voice-envs\kokoro-models` (nothing global). Its weights come from the kokoro-onnx GitHub release (`kokoro-v1.0.onnx` SHA-256 `7d5df8ec...a6c5`, `voices-v1.0.bin` SHA-256 `bca610b8...f9d`). Measured on CPU here: real-time factor 0.71 to 1.04 for a 24 to 29 s paragraph, so sentence-by-sentence playback would barely keep ahead of the speaker without a GPU or warm-start tuning. Kokoro has no Turkish voice. The first blind page is in `voice-listening/blind-english-1/blind-test.html`; **it has not been listened to or rated.** Not included yet: Piper (already judged unnatural), Chatterbox (CPU too slow; waits for a free GPU), Edge natural voices and any cloud voice (they send the text out and wait for the researcher's say-so).

**Decision rule.** Highest blind rating among candidates that meet criteria 4 and 5 for the intended use; if the best sample is a cloud voice, it ships as an opt-in provider with the existing "text is sent to a speech service" notice, and the device voice stays the fallback.

## Slices

Each slice is committed when its tests pass, as before.

**L0. Board script (`board_schema: 1`).** A lecture is an ordered list of steps; each step has a `say` (the spoken sentence) and zero or more `do` actions that start when that sentence starts: `write` (text in the handwriting font), `math` (TeX written left to right), `draw` (named strokes of an SVG diagram), `animate` (a declared widget or a rendered clip, with its prediction prompt), `highlight`, `erase`, `pause-for-question`. Cues bind to sentence boundaries, never to wall-clock time, so a slow voice cannot desynchronise the board. Stored in session state beside the lesson; the written lecture text remains the source of truth, and the board is a rendering of it.

**L1. The blackboard.** A board panel in the runtime: chalk-white and pastel strokes on a dark slate, grain, a chalk-tip cursor, writing revealed by a moving mask, KaTeX formulas revealed token by token, diagrams drawn by stroke-dash animation, erase with a smudge. Honors reduced motion by showing finished states. Handwriting font: a candidate from Caveat, Patrick Hand or Kalam (all listed on Google Fonts as SIL OFL; **licence and Latin-extended coverage to be verified**, then bundled and added to `dependency-lock.json`). Readability test: the researcher reads formulas and sentences at normal distance. Not verified until then.

**L2. Voice provider and playback queue.** `speak(sentence) -> audio, duration`; the page plays sentence by sentence, starting after the first sentence is ready, and fires the cues. Providers are detected, never required; with none, the device voice speaks and the board still plays. A short listening panel puts candidates side by side on the same English lecture paragraph for the researcher to rate before one becomes the default.

**L3. Live loop on the board.** After a quiz or probe cycle closes, the tutor gate opens a lecture block. The agent appends board steps while it teaches (`board-append`), the page plays them as they arrive. `Raise hand` pauses at the next sentence boundary; the question is recorded as an open question with the board position it was asked at; the answer is a new board segment (speech, writing, drawing) inserted at that point, and playback resumes where it stopped. Everything recorded as now: no evidence, no mastery.

**L4. Self-sufficient lectures.** A lint check: every concept in a lecture is explained on the board and in speech; external links are marked `supplementary`, never referenced as "see the link" in the spoken script. Existing teaching-card lint and spoken-script rules (formulas in words, language spans) are reused.

**L5. Spoken questions.** Push-to-talk, local Whisper transcription, shown to the researcher before sending. Turkish and other languages follow the English path through the same provider interface.
